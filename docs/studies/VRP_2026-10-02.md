# Volatility risk premium: selling SPX option insurance (2026-10-02)

Pre-registration: `docs/studies/vrp_prereg.json` (commit 63731d3, before results). Script: `tools/studies/vrp_study.py`.
Data: CBOE's free daily index histories (PUT, WPUT, BXM, CNDR, PPUT, VIX, VIX3M, SPX) and Yahoo ^SP500TR and ^IRX, through 2026-10-01.

## Bottom line

**The premium exists, but the trades that sell it earn no alpha after 2007. Promotion FAILS.** Selling insurance is a lower-volatility way to own stocks, not an edge.

- **Q1, the premium exists:** at month end, VIX exceeded the next 21 sessions' realized SPX vol in 84% of 440 months (1990–2026), by +4.1 vol points on average and similarly in every period. The misses are huge and arrive together: Feb 2020 −54, Sep 2008 −43, Aug 2008 −34, Mar 2025 −25.
- **Q2, it is not separate from beta.** Monthly excess returns regressed on SPX total-return excess (Newey-West):

| Index | early | middle | recent | pooled alpha (t) | beta | Sharpe vs SPX-TR (pooled) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **PUT** monthly ATM put-write | +3.2%/yr (2007-07–12) | −1.1% (2013–19) | −0.1% (2020–26) | **+0.0%/yr (0.0)** | 0.60 | 0.57 vs 0.65 |
| WPUT weekly put-write | +3.0% (2006–12) | −3.8% | −5.3% (t −2.2) | −1.9% (−1.5) | 0.53 | 0.36 vs 0.67 |
| BXM covered call | +1.6% (2002–06) | −0.1% / −0.6% | −1.9% | −0.8% (−0.7) | 0.63 | 0.46 vs 0.61 |
| CNDR iron condor | **+4.9% (1987–2006, t 3.2)** | −0.7% | −1.7% | +1.7% (1.5) | 0.11 | 0.41 vs 0.62 |
| PPUT *buying* protection | −2.6% (1987–2006, t −2.5) | −0.7% | +0.4% | −1.3% (−1.2) | 0.75 | 0.46 vs 0.62 |

- The pattern holds for every structure. Insurance was overpriced before about 2007: CNDR earned +4.9%/yr with almost no stock exposure, and buyers (PPUT) lost 2.6%/yr. Since then, sellers earn nothing beyond their stock exposure and buyers lose nothing. The edge was competed away after it became well known. The weekly put-write, the most "harvest every week" design, is the worst recently (−5.3%/yr, t −2.2).
- Every selling index falls less than SPX in a crash (PUT max drawdown −37% vs −55%) but trails it badly in bull markets (2013–19 PUT +7.1%/yr vs SPX-TR +14.7%). Hence: a lower-vol stock holding.
- **Q3 timing (info only):** selling only in contango (VIX3M > VIX) lifts PUT alpha to +1.1%/yr (t 0.95). Selling only when VIX > trailing realized vol gives +0.9%/yr (t 0.6). Neither is significant.
- **Q4 (retail cost on SPY options): not run.** With pooled alpha about zero, any spread makes it negative. Notes for scale: one SPY put needs about $77k of cash collateral, and the CBOE indexes assume fills with no spread or commission.

## Why overnight worked and this doesn't

Overnight still shows up out of sample in the desk's own data (+16 bp a night). The option premium shows up in VIX vs realized vol, but its tradable form stopped paying beyond beta about 20 years ago. A published, scalable, institutionally-harvested premium is the most crowded kind there is. That suggests the remaining candidates (forced index flows, a fair-universe 12-1 momentum) should be judged by how hard they are for large money to harvest, not only by how well documented they are.
