## 4. Starting-stop test (why the 1% starting stop stays)

The sweep: starting stop 0.35 / 0.5 / 0.75 / 1.0% × trail 0.25–0.5%, on the real fills and the one-arm entries, at all prices and at $20+. **1% was the best starting stop in every set.** Tighter stops get hit by noise: 44–56% of trades take the full stop at 0.35%, vs 14–28% at 1%. The best setup is one-arm entries at $20+ with a 1% starting stop, at **+0.065% per trade before costs**, positive in both halves (t ≈ 2.4). Net that's −0.045% at 0.11% cost, and −0.14% at 0.20%.

## 5. What we learned on 09-23 (corrections included)

- **The names are a coin flip once admitted.** A random minute after admission goes +1% before −1% 51.5% of the time, with no drift. An earlier "56% and drifting up" figure was hindsight: it counted time from before the name was admitted, when it was already running.
- **No timing trigger beats a random minute.** Breakout, VWAP reclaim, VWAP pullback, opening-range breakout, higher lows, strength vs SPY and heating all come in at or below random. Opening-range breakout looked like a 74.6% winner until the same hindsight was removed.
- **No seed-time feature picks better names** than what the book already admits (53.5% vs 48.7%).
- **Exit shape can't make the average positive.** Every stop and trail combination nets 0 to +0.06% before costs. Exits decide the win rate, not the average.
- **Costs are the capture bottleneck, and the cost is the spread.** Against the true bid/ask (SIP), orders fill at the touch, with nothing beyond it. So the lever is choosing tight-spread names, not order type.
- **Daily trend and news don't improve timing, but they do pick names.** Gapped down more than 1%: 46.0% all day (z −3.7). Above the 50-day average with news since the close: 57.8% and +0.09% at 30 minutes (z +2.3, both halves), a possible future book-ranking input. News in the last 60 minutes made one-arm entries worse (46.6%).
