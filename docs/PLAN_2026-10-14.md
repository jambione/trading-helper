## Operator direction (2026-10-14)
The desk is meant to **trade frequently, with several positions open at once and a full, fresh book from 09:45.**
"Lose the least by trading the least" is not the goal. On paper, a busy book is how evidence arrives: at about
12 fills a session every lever has died underpowered (memory: live-config-churns-faster-than-evidence). The work is
to make each trade worth taking, not to take fewer of them.

Live config as of `73217e4`: 09:45 start, premarket seating, 45 s scans, 5 slots × 20% (5% open risk), no-progress
180 s, breakeven 0.15% + 4 s decay + overtake, profit capture off, square + pre-square arms.

## 1. The pipeline and where it breaks

### Stage 1: finding names (THE bottleneck)
The screens rank by how much a name has *already* moved, so names reach the board after their run.
- ~~Momentum names gained +37 bp gross before any source seeded them~~ **Withdrawn 10/14:** that figure is measured only on names that were *later* seeded, and they were seeded because they rose, so it is hindsight (the same flaw as the 5-minutes-earlier sweep). It does not show a catchable earlier window; only a causal discovery rule on the full universe can (the news study is one).
- The board fades **−8.6 bp per 10 minutes**; refused candidates fade only −1.3 (`BOARD_NAMES_2026-10-14.md`).
- Names already up 2.35% in the first hour fade harder than the rest.

The target is to fill the book earlier **in the name's move**, not just earlier on the clock. Faster scans and
premarket seating find the same already-moved names a little sooner.

Ideas, all on free data:
1. **News-triggered seating (started 10/14).** Seat a name the minute a headline lands on the Alpaca news feed,
   before the move reaches the movers lists. Prior: above-50-day + news drifts up (memory: daily-context-picks-names-not-timing).
2. ~~Volume first, price second.~~ **Already tested (closed 10/14):** round 4 (9/26) ran ~430 volume / spread / pullback cells on free SIP, none cleared costs; volume pace predicts movement, not direction (surges up 21.5% / down 20.6%); the live pace gate graded worse (10/1). Volume only as a pairing with news or sector moves.
3. **Sector lead-lag.** When a sector ETF moves, seat the laggards before they catch up (pairs, candidate 6 in the
   10/9 indicator audit).
4. **Weight the sources that hold up.** Trending and AGY picks were the least bad; momentum was worst in both halves
   (−36 / −26 bp per 10 min). Pre-register dropping or down-weighting momentum.

### Stage 2: keeping the book full (capacity)
Seats go stream-silent, drop and are refused re-entry (memory: watchlist-bleeds-out-midmorning). The real-time feed
caps us near 50 names (memory: finnhub-50-subs-hangs-the-dashboard). A full, fresh book needs faster rotation of
stale seats and slots given to names with fresh volume or a fresh catalyst.

### Stage 3: the trigger (not the bottleneck)
Every arm tested matches a random minute on the same name. The trigger cannot beat the name it is given. The one
open trigger test is the %R 10-minute trend filter (`wr_trend_entry_prereg.json`, reads at 30 sessions from 10/12;
built, off live). Today "rising" is a one-minute slope, so a sideways line that ticks up counts as rising.

### Stage 4: cost per trade (the lever for a busy book)
About 3 bp per round trip on tight names. A busy book pays it on every trade, so frequency only pays once entries
stop crossing the spread. G2 (order-cost arms: limit at bid / mid vs crossing) reads **10/15**.

### Stage 5: exits (done well; cannot carry the mean)
The tight trail does its job: 1–3 minute holds are the only profitable bucket in October (+$6, 47% winners); 10+
minute holds are the worst (−$40, −22 bp). Exits own the win rate, not the mean.
